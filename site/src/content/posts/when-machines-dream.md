For decades, storytellers have grappled with a recurring question: *what happens when the tools we build to serve us begin to outgrow us?*

Across film, music, and literature, a consistent pattern emerges — the created thing that surpasses its creator, the system that optimizes beyond its intended boundaries, the intelligence that redefines the humans who made it.

This cultural anxiety is not accidental. It reflects genuine philosophical tensions that AI researchers, ethicists, and policymakers now grapple with in earnest: questions of value alignment, the concentration of decision-making power, and the erosion of human agency in automated systems.

These stories matter not because fiction predicts the future, but because it shapes how we *think about* the future — and thinking clearly about AI risk is one of the most important things we can do right now.
